BURNOUT’s story is based on a plot twist, one that will be revealed in the last moments of the last “Origins of Delusion” chapter in Part Three: Convergence. While this project reveals a lot of information on narrative devices, characters and subtext, the plot twist remains, to this day, locked away - even , who plays the Id character, is not aware of it.

It’s not a plot twist of a superficial nature; what you see is what you get across the entire story. But, it will pull the carpet beneath your feet on what you thought everything meant. The story includes intentional breadcrumbs that will only make sense as breadcrumbs on a second read.

So, the entire story is a misdirection.

There are three key elements to the story’s plot twist.

First of all, the characters themselves.

A previous author’s note confirmed that all of them, outside representing dramatic manifestations of Freud’s thesis, are intentional narrative devices. The Ego - the story’s absurd protagonist - is a MacGuffin device paired with the Unreliable Narrator technique.

So the Ego takes us through his memories of the backpacking trip for a reason; as he drives the story forward, a factual third-person reporting of events begins to clash with his own skewed recounting.

The Id is a Red Herring - a misdirection. And the Superego is a Foreshadowing device, signalling on the things to come. As secondary characters, they are extremely prone to and extremely revealing of the Ego’s MacGuffin leading up to the reveal of the plot twist in the final “Origins of Delusion” chapter.

Second, the pace of the story itself.

Although the prologue is a metaphorical slap in the face for audiences - in par with modern age storytelling, where the exposition starts at > 50% the intensity of action for that illusive attention grab - the rest of the chapters go through the backpacking trip in two intertwined modes.

Hate it, meh it or love it, the opening scene of 2024’s Bad Boys: Ride or Die is one of the best deliveries of this new kind of right-in-the-action expositions.

Martin Lawrence kills it - and it’s just a freaking hot dog.

The Ego’s account, akin to the superficial nature of social media but more absurd (think someone actually saying how awesome they are with their summer posts on social media) versus what actually happened.

This literary choice is also reflected on the mood setting videos, which intentionally blur the line between them. It’s an internal battle, externalised in text and audiovisual format. A battle with a meaning that the plot twist will redefine.

Just take a look at the logline’s third - sorry three - sentences…

He backpacked to fix burnout. But burnout was just a battlefield. To heal, he must end the war.

And finally, the biggest hint lies in the creative choice itself of tackling the story as a deconstructing project with the goal of up-skilling. This one is almost a dead giveaway, the intentionality in unfolding a story within a meta-story about creating the story in the first place.
