BURNOUT is an experimental series created by George Kary, born out of a passion for tackling multi-disciplinary and cross-functional projects. The purpose of the series is to explore:

Cinematography and storytelling. Techniques and principles, writing itself, lighting, editing, photography - you name it.

Long-form content and short-form format. Also fiddling with the rising trends of micro-learning and micro-drama.

Philosophical exercise in multimedia mode. Essentially exploring Freud’s concept of the Ego, Id and Superego and the idea that internal misalignment is what actually causes the thing we call “burnout”.

Follow the experiment.

The entire attempt, spanning from September 2025 to summer of 2026, led to leveraging all these learnings into a professional offering, as part of the human-heavy element of handling launches, pivots and AI adoption.

George is the founder of Blackaft Associates which offers a centralised management and delivery collective of biztech experts for startups and SMEs.

Across that period, multiple rounds of tests and iterations took place, including post-mortem analysis of social media metrics.

AI was heavily leveraged for augmenting research, testing and compiling a unified context for production. No content was generated by AI, in alignment with the human-for-humans principle.

So, all the theory and practice that went into creating, testing and finally launching the series, was, in essence, about rapid up-skilling.

Story.

The story is based on George’s own 3-month backpacking trip across Greece and Italy, back in 2025, when he decided to double-down on his new direction.

It’s, of course, over-dramatised to help achieve his exploration goals.

The Ego reminisces about the trip, as proof that it fixed burnout, once again taking charge of what was, up to that point, a fractured identity.

However, the Id and the Superego remain misaligned with the Ego’s self-rationalisations. As the Ego peels layer after layer to prove its victory, the full-fledged civil war surfaces between the 3 sides; an inner turmoil that no amount of analysis, travel or even emotional retrospection can resolve.

Except the tool of self-realisation, which everybody avoids out of shame and insecurity when it comes to confronting the reality of their selves, choices and environments, and not their dressed up versions.

The story takes place across 104 days, mostly in reverse chronological order. This particular choice of narrative format helps express the unravelling of the false assumptions that we all hold based on a preferential and skewed view of our memories; or rather, how we choose to interpret those memories.

Thus, the narrative was created in such a way to intentionally expose the entire story’s layers, one by one:

The travel layer - oh, a guy who went backpacking to feel good

The emotional layer - oh, he felt really bad

The identity layer - oh, he was lying to himself all along

In summary, the series hopes to help viewers resonate with their own truths (and layers) by presenting the entire story with such raw brutality that it contrasts heavily with the content presented online and especially on social media.

Making it so uncomfortable and infuriating to watch someone being so publicly absurd that it triggers cognitive dissonance with your own private absurdity.

And making you not being able to stop watching to fill the gap.

Structure.

3 parts, 19 chapters total. Each chapter is complemented with a short-form video, meant to help set the mood for viewers.

Then you read the chapter.

That’s it.
