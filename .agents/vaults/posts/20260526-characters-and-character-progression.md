BURNOUT’s primary character is the Ego, played by George Kary. The story begins with the Ego being absurdly obnoxious, arrogant, superior and patronising (sometimes breaking the fourth wall for dramatic effect), looking back and remembering a 103-day backpacking trip, feasting on its victory over burnout and its accomplishments. As the story unfolds, its assumptions and certainty appear and feel hollow. The Ego begins to unravel and spiral, ultimately forced to face the secondary characters at the end of the series.

Follow the experiment.

The secondary characters are the Id and the Superego, loosely inspired by Freud’s fundamental principles on human identity. They are silent, interfering with the Ego’s delusions, as both of them have been excluded from the Ego’s understanding of the self. As the story unfolds, both attempt to drive their separate agendas against each other, taking hits, until they are also forced to face the primary character for the final realization (plot twist reveal).

The Ego is the only narrator both in the teaser videos and the text chapters, rambling incessantly and driving the story forward (MacGuffin device, also paired with the Unreliable Narrator narrative device).

Secondary characters.

The Id represents the raw, sensual, primal side of the self, seeking pleasure, comfort and the “good old days”. It represents the past. The narrative intentionally paints the Id as the victim of the entire series. It’s a Red Herring narrative device (misdirection).

~ rim chroma = blue

The Superego represents the judgemental figure and moral compass, relentlessly pushing forward, offering no room or rest. It represents the future. It’s also a Foreshadowing device meant to offset the Red Herring Id and the Unreliable Narrator Ego, as it looks clearly menacing and the “bad guy” in the story.

~ rim chroma = red, opposite light/dark sides to the Id for lighting

Emotional states.

Photography stills from one of the initial discovery sessions for the Superego’s emotional states, in an effort to POC the series’s character progression.

Both secondary characters undergo 6 different emotional states (+1 exclusively reserved for the ending sequence and the plot twist reveal).

Part One: Dissonance ~ represents Act 1 of the entire series

Exposition ~ an obnoxious and analytical Ego with a determined Superego and a playful Id (both dark T-Shirt)

Inciting Incident ~ Ego turns aggressive, both Superego and Id become uncertain and in disbelief (dark undershirt)

Part Two: Resonance ~ Act 2 of the entire series

Rising Action ~ Ego is now troubled and while both are concerned, the Superego is plotting and the Id appears deep in thought (grey T-Shirt)

Midpoint ~ Ego replaces its analytical mode with an emotional stance going defensive, the Superego looks cunning and the Id annoyed (grey undershirt)

Part Three: Convergence ~ Act 3

Pre-Climax ~ determined Ego, victimised Superego, furious Id (white T-Shirt)

Climax ~ controlling, furious and victimised (white undershirt).

Resolution ~ no spoilers (duh)

Technical aspects.

Both secondary characters wear a beanie across all states.

All photo and video material used across all methods and channels showcase both characters above the chest with the collarbone and just enough of the shoulders-to-forearms ratio to achieve the “part of one” symbolism.

The colors, layers of clothes and glitching and trilateral sequence appearances of both secondary characters represent a Chekhov’s Gun narrative device (vital clues for introducing, expanding and revealing upon the core identity layer).
