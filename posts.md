BURNOUT is an experimental series \- the process of making it is equally \- if not more \- important as the actual story itself. After all, it is an exercise in upskilling on various fields and an effort born out of a passion for tackling multi\-disciplinary and cross\-functional projects ([continues](https://burnoutseries.substack.com/p/welcome-aboard)).

But let’s get back to the process.

---

---

## The Story

Deconstructing a seemingly superficial story of travel and injecting a little bit of drama into it (103 days of backpacking across 3 countries) still kept the surface level at the center of the narrative.

It had no grit. No backbone.

That’s where the concept of burnout came in: the emotional layer beneath all this fancy scenery and drone shots. It marked the beginning of a conflict.

And there’s really no good story without a conflict.

But that wasn’t enough. Feeling restless after years of not taking a vacation? Big deal. Undoing your life and embracing solo travel? There’s no resonance for viewers and readers alike \- hell, there’s no resonance for the creator, either.

So, the more analytical the deconstruction of the story became \- and the more testing on social media affirmed these assumptions of “bleh”, the more organic it felt that there was a deeper layer, the core layer.

Freud’s civil war ([cited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Id,_ego_and_superego)) \- the bullshit we tell ourselves.

Now that, will absolutely resonate. And it did in closed testing.

But in order for it to work, the narrative needs to completely embrace absurdity, maintain an anti\-mainstream attitude across the entire story and point the middle finger to the superficial beauty of social media.

It’s gotta make you uncomfortable, because the conflict is internal.

## The Characters

To offset the bluntness of the “travel documentary” effect, we needed to satisfy one more condition for effective storytelling.

We needed a hero for the conflict.

Or an anti\-hero ([ref](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking_Bad)).

This was easy \- having Freud’s work laid out in front of us, deciding on the 3 main characters was instant: the Id, the Ego and the Superego. What we had to figure out was the binding thread between them.

Sure, they’re arguing.

But why?

We had to connect the story’s concept of burnout with that internal misalignment we all feel at various parts in our lives. How the id craves pleasure and comfort; how the superego stands judgemental; and how the ego rationalises.

Now that last part \- the rationalisation \- began to resonate with the conflict and the story’s veil of absurdity.

You can tell who’s the asshole easily.

But there’s still something wrong, as we employ various narrative devices at most, if not all, unexpected places, setting the stage for the conflict’s climax and the ensuing plot\-twist resolution at the end.

A good story leaves an after\-taste; one that resonates personally.

So, we had the journey \- and we had the hero.

It was time to get back to the technical aspects, for without them, even a good story with good characters can suck.

## The Mechanics

First of all, simply to conclude the conjunction (and juxtaposition) of the story with the characters clashing, it just made good sense to put a simplifying label on the whole thing \- and thus, the 3 parts were born:

1. **Dissonance** \~ set in Italy, backwards
2. **Resonance** \~ set in Greece, forwards
3. **Convergence** \~ set again in Greece, but backwards (the last chapter is whimsically called Origins of Delusion)

Then we had to figure out lighting, composition, photography and videography from scratch (video editing is relatively easy to learn, in comparison). It was important to capture a similar Christopher Nolan vibe across all POVs, including Author’s Notes and BTS (inspired by Tenet’s ([cited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenet)) palette).

That’s new media and cinematic for you. The story itself feels like you’re in someone’s head, hence the intense colors, dead pans and center placed characters. The Ego is, well, a Netflix\-styled interview, but, absurd.

Then came theming, sound design and audio mixing. Nothing fancy, but the goal was to make something that’d be worth going back to years from now.

And finally, formats and channels for publishing the series (and the book).

It’s all pretty straightforward.

title: "Author's Note: Characters and character progression"
published: "2026-05-26T20:19:51Z"
url: "https://burnoutseries.substack.com/p/characters-and-character-progression"
---

BURNOUT’s primary character is the Ego, played by [George Kary](https://substack.com/@georgekary). The story begins with the Ego being absurdly obnoxious, arrogant, superior and patronising (sometimes breaking the fourth wall for dramatic effect), looking back and remembering a 103\-day backpacking trip, feasting on its victory over burnout and its accomplishments. As the story unfolds, its assumptions and certainty appear and feel hollow. The Ego begins to unravel and spiral, ultimately forced to face the secondary characters at the end of the series.

---

---

The secondary characters are the Id and the Superego, loosely inspired by Freud’s fundamental principles on human identity. They are silent, interfering with the Ego’s delusions, as both of them have been excluded from the Ego’s understanding of the self. As the story unfolds, both attempt to drive their separate agendas against each other, taking hits, until they are also forced to face the primary character for the final realization (plot twist reveal).

The Ego is the only narrator both in the teaser videos and the text chapters, rambling incessantly and driving the story forward (MacGuffin device, also paired with the Unreliable Narrator narrative device).

## Secondary characters.

- The Id represents the raw, sensual, primal side of the self, seeking pleasure, comfort and the “good old days”. It represents the past. The narrative intentionally paints the Id as the victim of the entire series. It’s a Red Herring narrative device (misdirection).

*\~ rim chroma \= blue*
- The Superego represents the judgemental figure and moral compass, relentlessly pushing forward, offering no room or rest. It represents the future. It’s also a Foreshadowing device meant to offset the Red Herring Id and the Unreliable Narrator Ego, as it looks clearly menacing and the “bad guy” in the story.

*\~ rim chroma \= red, opposite light/dark sides to the Id for lighting*

## Emotional states.

Both secondary characters undergo 6 different emotional states (\+1 exclusively reserved for the ending sequence and the plot twist reveal).

1. **Part One: Dissonance** \~ represents Act 1 of the entire series

	1. Exposition \~ an obnoxious and analytical Ego with a determined Superego and a playful Id (both dark T\-Shirt)
	2. Inciting Incident \~ Ego turns aggressive, both Superego and Id become uncertain and in disbelief (dark undershirt)
2. **Part Two: Resonance** \~ Act 2 of the entire series

	1. Rising Action \~ Ego is now troubled and while both are concerned, the Superego is plotting and the Id appears deep in thought (grey T\-Shirt)
	2. Midpoint \~ Ego replaces its analytical mode with an emotional stance going defensive, the Superego looks cunning and the Id annoyed (grey undershirt)
3. **Part Three: Convergence** \~ Act 3

	1. Pre\-Climax \~ determined Ego, victimised Superego, furious Id (white T\-Shirt)
	2. Climax \~ controlling, furious and victimised (white undershirt).
	3. Resolution \~ *no spoilers (duh)*

## Technical aspects.

Both secondary characters wear a beanie across all states.

All photo and video material used across all methods and channels showcase both characters above the chest with the collarbone and just enough of the shoulders\-to\-forearms ratio to achieve the “part of one” symbolism.

The colors, layers of clothes and glitching and trilateral sequence appearances of both secondary characters represent a Chekhov’s Gun narrative device (vital clues for introducing, expanding and revealing upon the core identity layer).

BURNOUT is a purely creative project, with text, images and videos exclusively created by humans, for humans, in alignment with Blackaft’s principles, the [biztech venture firm founded by George Kary](https://www.linkedin.com/in/georgekary/). But the project has long been augmented by AI chatbots; at first, it was ChatGPT and then it became Gemini. That is, to say, augmentation strictly focused on research, context building, transposing data and, of course, refining iterations through a post\-mortem process of analysing human\-made content and metrics.

This is all just a fancy way to say humans make stuff by using AI before and after the humans\-making\-stuff process takes place.

---

---

Balancing both sides of the aisle has been a massive hurdle, especially in terms of record keeping. You know how it goes: you open a conversation on ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, etc., only to have the damn thing locked in that particular session, essentially unavailable to all other chats.

Naturally tackling launches, pivots and AI adoption, Blackaft employed an AI\-augmentation\-first approach from the start.

It also meant that launches, pivots and especially AI adoption may and will require human creativity, which is essentially what this series gradually became across the many months that George Kary has been going at it.

A practice run.

## Blackaft Studio.

BURNOUT now lives under <https://github.com/blackaft>, with a little bit of Google Drive and Google Photos for handling multimedia.

For the devs/biztech folk out there, the setup \- and Blackaft’s philosophy \- is generally agnostic in terms of the tool or model, as a core foundational premise to be able to serve clients by embedding into their own operations to more effectively tackle launches and pivots. Since the repository also adheres to the firm’s central infrastructure direction, the cost to initialise such a thing with a VS Code \+ Cline \+ BYOK via Google’s Agent Platform setup was a meagre $0\.98\.

Mind you, the agent was on auto\-pilot. It just received a single command, and 3\-4 additional prompts that were specific to the Burnout Series.

The process is also self\-improving; meaning there are deterministic workflows inherent to the agentic setup focused on iterating minor improvements the more data the repository builds (i.e. knowledge bases or LLM Vaults).

Suffice to say, Gemini remains the king across benchmarking in terms of handling context for such type of processes in building up blocks on top of blocks.

## Results.

Why does this entire thing matter? Sure, it sounds fancy; a creative project using such a “coding” level of AI?

Outside helping with the actual production content and schedule, this post was crafted by a human in less than 10 minutes. The thoughts and raw notes were already there since September 2025\. The AI, operating inside that Blackaft Studio agentic setup, was able to provide the human with all the necessary context.

And also, every post now has a prompt. Made by machines, for machines. This is, after all, all about embracing the world’s impending duality of the extremes.

Machines on the back; humans at the front.

title: "Author's Note: Introduction"
published: "2026-05-17T12:51:25Z"
url: "https://burnoutseries.substack.com/p/welcome-aboard"
---

BURNOUT is an experimental series created by [George Kary](https://substack.com/@georgekary), born out of a passion for tackling multi\-disciplinary and cross\-functional projects. The purpose of the series is to explore:

1. **Cinematography and storytelling**. Techniques and principles, writing itself, lighting, editing, photography \- you name it.
2. **Long\-form content and short\-form format**. Also fiddling with the rising trends of micro\-learning and micro\-drama.
3. **Philosophical exercise in multimedia mode**. Essentially exploring Freud’s concept of the Ego, Id and Superego and the idea that internal misalignment is what actually causes the thing we call “burnout”.

---

---

The entire attempt, spanning from September 2025 to summer of 2026, led to leveraging all these learnings into a professional offering, as part of the human\-heavy element of handling launches, pivots and AI adoption.

*[George is the founder of Blackaft Associates](https://www.linkedin.com/in/georgekary/) which offers a centralised management and delivery collective of biztech experts for startups and SMEs.*

Across that period, multiple rounds of tests and iterations took place, including post\-mortem analysis of social media metrics.

AI was heavily leveraged for augmenting research, testing and compiling a unified context for production. **No content was generated by AI**, in alignment with the human\-for\-humansprinciple.

So, all the theory and practice that went into creating, testing and finally launching the series, was, in essence, about **rapid up\-skilling**.

## Story.

The story is based on George’s own 3\-month backpacking trip across Greece and Italy, back in 2025, when he decided to double\-down on his new direction.

It’s, of course, over\-dramatised to help achieve his exploration goals.

> The Ego reminisces about the trip, as proof that it fixed burnout, once again taking charge of what was, up to that point, a fractured identity.
>
> However, the Id and the Superego remain misaligned with the Ego’s self\-rationalisations. As the Ego peels layer after layer to prove its victory, the full\-fledged civil war surfaces between the 3 sides; an inner turmoil that no amount of analysis, travel or even emotional retrospection can resolve.
>
> Except the tool of self\-realisation, which everybody avoids out of shame and insecurity when it comes to confronting the reality of their selves, choices and environments, and not their dressed up versions.

The story takes place across 104 days, mostly in reverse chronological order. This particular choice of narrative format helps express the unravelling of the false assumptions that we all hold based on a preferential and skewed view of our memories; or rather, how we choose to interpret those memories.

Thus, the narrative was created in such a way to intentionally expose the entire story’s layers, one by one:

1. The travel layer \- *oh, a guy who went backpacking to feel good*
2. The emotional layer \- *oh, he felt really bad*
3. The identity layer \- *oh, he was lying to himself all along*

In summary, the series hopes to help viewers resonate with their own truths (and layers) by presenting the entire story with such raw brutality that it contrasts heavily with the content presented online and especially on social media.

Making it so uncomfortable and infuriating to watch someone being so publicly absurd that it triggers cognitive dissonance with your own private absurdity.

And making you not being able to stop watching to fill the gap.

## Structure.

3 parts, 19 chapters total. Each chapter is complemented with a short\-form video, meant to help set the mood for viewers.

Then you read the chapter.

That’s it.
